# Protocol 3: examples and experimental audio

## Example Catalogue

The Example Catalogue is a close-reading resource rather than a machine-generated result table. It contains examples selected to illustrate different analytical mechanisms and explicitly grades their evidential status.

The catalogue includes categories that the terminal inspection tool intentionally does not decide, including:

- exact lexical repetition;
- strict terminal recurrence;
- broad assonance and consonantal fields;
- medial/internal echoes;
- accent-sensitive correspondences;
- junctional or composite effects;
- formulaic and rhetorical recurrence across larger spans.

The distinction is deliberate: a terminal matching algorithm should not silently classify every perceptible sound relation as the same phenomenon.

## Experimental rhythmic realizations

The audio files are preliminary rhythmic realizations intended to make selected grouping, recurrence, accentual, and cadential relations easier to hear in continuous performance. Minor variations in timing, pitch, and stress belong to the provisional performances. The supplied transcriptions give the intended reading rather than a time-aligned record of every sung detail.

They are **not** presented as:

- reconstructions of an ancient melody;
- demonstrations of a historically transmitted meter;
- independent proof of poetic status;
- evidence that the Cubase project tempo or meter belonged historically to the text.

Where Cubase tempo/meter settings are reported, they describe only the supplied experimental realization.

The Example Catalogue is not intended as an exhaustive index of audio files. A recording is mentioned there only when it directly accompanies an analytical example. Additional demonstrative recordings may therefore be added to `audio/` without requiring a new Catalogue entry unless they introduce a substantively new analytical example.

## Genesis 1:1–5

The Genesis recording is a Torah case-study realization. Its listening notes draw attention to local lexical echo, repeated divine-name framing, recurring sound fields, and cadential return.

## Exodus 13:1–10

The Exodus recording is a Torah case-study realization of a longer continuous unit. Its listening notes draw attention to lexical and formulaic return, exact repeated syntax, second-person suffixal concentration, and longer-range returns across successive verses. The corresponding Catalogue entry expands the earlier Exod. 13.10 example rather than creating a separate audio-only case.

## Ana BeKoach

The *Ana BeKoach* recording is a comparative orientation example and is not part of the Torah corpus. Its compact, dense recurrence makes several auditory mechanisms unusually easy to hear. It is included as perceptual orientation before the more distributed biblical cases, not as evidence for Torah-wide claims.

## Descriptive distribution visualization

The repository also contains a default STRICT descriptive distribution visualization generated by the separate distribution tool. The visualization protocol, exact defaults, sensitivity options, window-level values, and reproducibility hashes are documented in `04_DISTRIBUTION_VISUALIZATION.md`.

The catalogue, audio examples, and distribution visualization are independent repository resources. They may be cited, reused, or regenerated separately from any particular publication.
