# Torah Poetic Organization: Example Catalogue

Analytical purpose, examples, and evidential status

Repository companion

## How to Read This Catalogue

The catalogue is organized by analytical purpose. Every entry states why the example matters for a study of poetic organization in the Torah, what it demonstrates, and how its evidential weight should be understood.

Most material comes from the Pentateuch. A small comparative section at the end uses Psalms and later liturgy as auditory calibration, making particular sound mechanisms especially easy to hear before subtler Torah cases are considered.

Evidence is graded. Exact lexical repetition, received phrase or verse closure, and strict stress-defined terminal recurrence are relatively constrained. Medial, junctional, composite, and broad sound-field relations are more interpretive and are labelled accordingly.

## Source and Transcription Audit

All Torah references and displayed Torah forms in this version were checked against the repository's frozen Sefaria snapshot, version 'Tanach with Ta'amei Hamikra'. Display transcription follows the project's pronunciation-oriented conventions rather than the deterministic repository transliteration; known differences between the two are stated only where they affect analysis.

Psalm 40.8-9 and Psalm 104.4-5 were checked against Miqra According to the Masorah (MAM) on Sefaria. Ana Be-koach was checked against Sefaria's Siddur Ashkenaz text; liturgical wording and pronunciation can vary by rite.

Biblical references use chapter.verse style (Gen. 1.1-5). Repository command-line ranges may still use chapter:verse syntax because that is the tool's input format.

## Evidence Labels

CORE - Strong case used to establish or directly test the analytical framework.

SUPPORTING - Additional example illustrating a defined mechanism.

TEXTUAL-FORM - Case whose interest depends on qere/ketiv or another marked textual form.

EXPLORATORY - Perception-dependent or more interpretive proposal retained for testing.

ORIENTATION - Non-Pentateuchal example used only to make an auditory mechanism easier to recognize.

## Transcription and Repository Relation

The passage-inspection tool audits word-final correspondence pairs under explicit parameters but does not decide literary function. It also does not capture every medial, junctional, composite, rhetorical, or long-range relation in this catalogue.

The current STRICT terminal protocol is narrower than the catalogue's full descriptive vocabulary. A conspicuous auditory relation that fails the STRICT definition is therefore labelled more narrowly - for example as a broader echo, medial relation, or exploratory correspondence - rather than silently counted as strict rhyme.

## 1. Framing and Marked Torah Forms

The opening entries establish two distinct points. Deut. 31.19 provides a textual frame in which writing, teaching, oral placement, and the repeated phrase ha-shira ha-zot converge. The following marked-form cases ask a narrower question: can a received qere or an unusual textual form also participate in local sound organization without implying that the form arose for poetic reasons?

### 1.1 Deuteronomy 31.19 - writing, teaching, and ha-shira ha-zot

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Deut. 31.19
**Mechanism:** parallel imperatives; repeated framing phrase; oral-transmission language

**Why this example is here.** Deut. 31.19 is an especially suggestive framing text for the hypothesis explored in this project. Writing, teaching, oral placement ('put it in their mouths'), and the repeated designation ha-shira ha-zot occur within one compact unit, linking textual transmission and performed speech through the language of shira.

```text
Ve-atta kitvu lakhem et ha-shira ha-zot,
ve-lammedah et bnei Yisrael,
simah be-fihem,
lema'an tihyeh li ha-shira ha-zot
le-ed bivnei Yisrael.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Three imperatives - kitvu, lammedah, simah - move from writing to teaching to placing the text 'in their mouths'. The repeated ha-shira ha-zot frames that movement and gives the unit a readily segmentable rhetorical shape.

### 1.2 Leviticus 25.30 - qere lo within a dense lo-chain

**Status:** TEXTUAL-FORM
**Reference:** Lev. 25.30
**Mechanism:** qere/ketiv; exact lexical repetition

**Why this example is here.** The example asks whether the performed qere of the received text can participate in local sound patterning even when the consonantal level is different.

```text
ve-im lo yiga'el ...
ad-melot lo shanah temimah ...
asher lo choma ...
lo yetze ba-yovel
```

**What it demonstrates.** The received qere in asher lo choma joins a dense series of lo forms already present in the verse, so the read form participates in the audible chain.

**Interpretive note.** The example concerns the audible function of the received qere within the verse; the qere/ketiv distinction itself remains a separate textual fact.

### 1.3 Deuteronomy 21.15-16 - senu'ah, ha-senu'ah, la-seni'ah | ve-hayah

**Status:** TEXTUAL-FORM
**Reference:** Deut. 21.15-16
**Mechanism:** marked textual form; cross-verse boundary linkage

**Why this example is here.** The interest lies in the sequence of forms, not merely in the final resemblance. The ordinary senu'ah form occurs twice in v. 15; the verse then closes with la-seni'ah, and v. 16 immediately opens ve-hayah.

```text
... ve-ha-achat senu'ah ...
... ha-ahuvah ve-ha-senu'ah ...
ve-hayah ha-ben ha-bekhor la-seni'ah |
ve-hayah bayom hanchilo et-banav ...
```

**What it demonstrates.** The progression senu'ah -> ha-senu'ah -> la-seni'ah is followed at the textual boundary by ve-hayah. The marked verse-final form therefore participates in a boundary-spanning auditory linkage.

**Interpretive note.** The analytical point is functional: in the received text, la-seni'ah closes the verse and immediately links acoustically to the following ve-hayah.

## 2. Basic Sound Techniques in Torah Passages

These entries move from the most constrained terminal identities to broader categories such as exact repetition, assonance, consonantal resonance, accentual correspondence, medial echo, and composite or junctional effects. The labels are intentionally not interchangeable.

### 2.1 Strict-compatible terminal recurrence - baseline examples

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Deut. 6.8; Gen. 1.1-2; Deut. 18.10-14
**Mechanism:** stress-defined terminal identity

**Why this example is here.** The catalogue needs a baseline category before introducing less direct relations. These examples use different lexemes whose stress-defined terminal signatures coincide without requiring extended consonantal equivalence or cross-word fusion.

```text
Deut. 6.8:       le-ot ... le-totafot
Gen. 1.1-2:      ha-shamayim ... ha-mayim
Deut. 18.10-14:  ba-esh ... u-menachesh ... ve-doresh ... yoresh
```

**What it demonstrates.** Straightforward terminal identities in -ot, -ayim, and -esh.

### 2.2 Exodus 14.29-30 - ba-yabashah | va-yosha as a broader echo

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Exod. 14.29-30
**Mechanism:** cross-verse auditory echo with different stress-defined signatures

**Why this example is here.** This conspicuous pair is useful precisely because the present STRICT protocol does not classify it as a full stress-defined rhyme. It illustrates the difference between a broader auditory resemblance and a computationally strict terminal match.

```text
... Yisrael halkhu ba-yabashah betokh ha-yam |
va-yosha A-donai bayom ha-hu ...
```

**What it demonstrates.** The -yabashah / -yosha resemblance becomes salient directly across the verse boundary.

**Interpretive note.** Under the current stress-defined protocol, ba-yabashah and va-yosha have different full terminal signatures; the relation is therefore classified as a broader cross-verse echo rather than a STRICT match.

### 2.3 Exodus 13.10 - miyamim yamima as a compact lexical-sound concentration

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Exod. 13.10
**Mechanism:** repeated lexical core; dominant y-m sequence

**Why this example is here.** This compact phrase shows how a repeated lexical/syllabic core can create a strong local acoustic concentration without needing an extended rhyme chain.

```text
ve-shamarta et ha-chuqqah ha-zot le-mo'adah,
mi-yamim yamimah.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Miyamim / yamimah repeats the y-m-m material and the yamim lexical core in immediate succession, making the phrase an economical example of dominant sound clustering.

### 2.4 Deuteronomy 11.17 - broad assonance rather than exact rhyme

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Deut. 11.17
**Mechanism:** final-vowel field; pronunciation sensitivity

**Why this example is here.** This example separates assonance from exact terminal rhyme: several important clause endings concentrate a similar low-vowel color even though their consonantal endings differ.

```text
ha-adamah ... yevulah ...
meherah ... ha-aretz ha-tovah
```

**What it demonstrates.** A broad /a/-dominant field near salient clause endings under the adopted reading.

**Interpretive note.** This is a qualitative assonance field rather than a STRICT terminal-rhyme class; pronunciation tradition affects its acoustic strength.

### 2.5 Genesis 6.9 - exact repetition as structural sound

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Gen. 6.9
**Mechanism:** exact lexical repetition / structural anchor

**Why this example is here.** Exact repetition should not automatically be discarded as trivial. Placement can turn a repeated name into an audible frame or anchor.

```text
Eleh toledot Noach,
Noach ish tzaddiq tamim hayah be-dorotav;
et ha-E-lohim hithalekh Noach.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Three occurrences of Noach keep the subject acoustically present across genealogy, description, and closing clause.

### 2.6 Genesis 1.1-2 - consonantal and prefixal texture

**Status:** CORE
**Reference:** Gen. 1.1-2
**Mechanism:** alliteration; recurrent onset; local sound texture

**Why this example is here.** The opening shows why sound organization cannot be reduced to terminal rhyme: repeated onsets and consonantal texture are already active before the chapter's larger formulas are considered.

```text
Bereshit
bara
veha'aretz
vavohu
vechoshekh
veruach
```

**What it demonstrates.** Bereshit / bara forms a b/r echo; ha'aretz and veruach extend the r-rich texture; recurrent ve-/va- onsets give successive phrases a related beginning.

**Interpretive note.** This entry concerns local sound texture; the stronger Genesis 1 case emerges when such texture converges with lexical return, formulaic recurrence, and phrase closure.

### 2.7 Numbers 21.9 - nechash / nechoshet / nashakh in one local sequence

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Num. 21.9
**Mechanism:** consonantal resonance inside one verse

**Why this example is here.** The n/ch/sh family is concentrated within one verse, making it a well-defined example of consonantal resonance rather than an abstract three-word list.

```text
Va-ya'as Moshe nechash nechoshet ...
ve-hayah im-nashakh ha-nachash et-ish,
ve-hibbit el-nechash ha-nechoshet va-chai.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Nechash / ha-nachash and nechoshet repeatedly share n-ch-sh material, while nashakh rearranges part of the same inventory.

**Interpretive note.** The relation is consonantal resonance rather than terminal rhyme.

### 2.8 Genesis 18.9-10 - received accentuation and elékha / ishtékha

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Gen. 18.9-10
**Mechanism:** Masoretically supported accentual correspondence

**Why this example is here.** This tests whether a correspondence dependent on prominence is supported by received accentuation rather than created by the analyst.

```text
Gen. 18.9:  ... Sarah ishtékha ...
Gen. 18.10: shov ashuv elékha ... le-Sarah ishtékha ...
```

**What it demonstrates.** Ishtékha is penultimate under the received accentuation; in v. 10 elékha likewise supplies a penultimate ending, so the echo does not require an invented stress shift.

### 2.9 Genesis 22.12, 18 - a weaker accentual return

**Status:** EXPLORATORY
**Reference:** Gen. 22.12, 18
**Mechanism:** accentual form entering a longer sound field

**Why this example is here.** This weaker case defines the boundary of the accentual category: inherited prominence may reinforce a distant echo, but distance and reduced similarity lower its evidential weight.

```text
Gen. 22.12: ... ki-yere E-lohim áta ...
Gen. 22.18: ... asher shamá'ta be-qoli.
```

**What it demonstrates.** A possible /ata/ return across the narrative.

### 2.10 Genesis 22.17 - junctional correspondence across a word boundary

**Status:** EXPLORATORY
**Reference:** Gen. 22.17
**Mechanism:** continuous-speech boundary effect

**Why this example is here.** The example asks whether oral continuity can make a relation perceptible across a word boundary rather than at a lexical ending.

```text
... s'fat | ha-yam ...
```

**What it demonstrates.** A possible junctional contribution to the wider Aqedah sound field in continuous delivery.

**Interpretive note.** The proposed effect belongs to continuous oral delivery across the word boundary and is therefore perceptual rather than orthographic.

### 2.11 Deuteronomy 11.14 - one word in two overlapping sound relations

**Status:** CORE
**Reference:** Deut. 11.14
**Mechanism:** medial lexical echo + terminal suffixal field

**Why this example is here.** A terminal-only analysis cannot see this mechanism: a morphologically complex word can carry an internal echo while its ending participates in a different chain.

```text
yoreh u-malqosh;
ve-asafta deganekha ve-tiroshekha ve-yitzharekha.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Internal tirosh- echoes malqosh, while the full tiroshekha continues into the surrounding -ekha field.

**Interpretive note.** The medial echo does not depend on shifting the principal stress. The close-reading form ve-tiroshekha and the repository form tiroshkha reflect different, explicitly documented transcription rules.

**Repository audit.** A frozen STRICT inspection output for Deut. 11.10-15 is included at results/passages/deuteronomy_11-10_11-15_strict/.

### 2.12 Genesis 22.1 - proposed composite closure toward hineni

**Status:** EXPLORATORY
**Reference:** Gen. 22.1
**Mechanism:** distributed partial cues before a stronger closing form

**Why this example is here.** This defines 'composite' correspondence as an explicitly interpretive category: several partial cues may accumulate even when no preceding word forms a direct rhyme with the closing word.

```text
Vayehi achar ha-devarim ha-eleh
ve-ha-E-lohim nisa et Avraham
va-yomer elav Avraham
va-yomer hineni.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Ha-eleh and nisa provide partial vowel/consonant cues before the stronger closing hineni.

**Interpretive note.** This is a cumulative, perception-dependent relation and is therefore kept distinct from direct terminal rhyme.

### 2.13 Genesis 37.1-2 - distributed field before echav

**Status:** EXPLORATORY
**Reference:** Gen. 37.1-2
**Mechanism:** distributed vowel/consonant field; proposed composite closure

**Why this example is here.** This second composite case asks whether a weakly distributed field can prepare a more explicit later recurrence.

```text
Vay-yeshev Ya'aqov be-eretz megurei aviv,
be-eretz Kena'an.
Eleh toldot Ya'aqov:
Yosef ben sheva-'esre shanah, hayah
ro'eh et echav ...
```

**What it demonstrates.** A possible field across yeshev, aviv, Kena'an, and the later echav, after which echav recurs more explicitly.

**Interpretive note.** This entry defines a possible perceptual mechanism for distributed sound fields; its status remains exploratory.

## 3. Parallelism and Recurrence across Larger Units

These cases shift from local sound to repeated formulas and rhetorical shapes that operate across larger textual spans.

### 3.1 Deuteronomy 6 and 11 - formulaic parallelism across related passages

**Status:** CORE
**Reference:** Deut. 6.7-9; 11.19-20
**Mechanism:** repeated instructional formulas across textual distance

**Why this example is here.** The passages share thematic ground, so the question is how stable wording and cadence reinforce continuity across separate units.

```text
be-shivtekha be-veitekha,
u-ve-lekhtekha ba-derekh,
u-ve-shokhbekha u-vqumekha.

U-khtavtam al mezuzot beitekha
u-vish'arekha.
```

**What it demonstrates.** The domestic-journey sequence and threshold-writing formula recur with closely matching wording.

### 3.2 Deuteronomy 25.17-19 and Numbers 14.8-9 - comparable rhetorical shape

**Status:** EXPLORATORY
**Reference:** Deut. 25.17-19; Num. 14.8-9
**Mechanism:** cross-passage rhetorical/cadential parallelism

**Why this example is here.** Because the passages are contextually unrelated, they provide a harder test of recurring short-clause and terminal-command shapes.

```text
Deut. 25:
ve-ata ayef ve-yagea,
ve-lo yare E-lohim.
...
Lo tishkach.

Num. 14:
akh ba-A-donai al timrodu,
ve-atem al tire'u et am ha'aretz ...
va-A-donai itanu.
Al tira'um.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Both units compress toward short terminal commands after longer clauses.

## 4. Extended Torah Case Files

These longer entries gather several mechanisms inside continuous passages and are therefore more important than isolated word pairs.

### 4.1 The Shema passages - compatible structures across three Torah units

**Status:** CORE
**Reference:** Deut. 6.4-9; 11.13-21; Num. 15.37-41
**Mechanism:** suffixal fields; formulaic return; medial echo; lexical framing

**Why this example is here.** The three passages later recited together provide a demanding test of formal compatibility across separate Torah units.

```text
Deut. 6:
Ve'ahavta et A-donai E-lohekha be-khol levavkha,
u-ve-khol nafshekha,
u-ve-khol me'odekha.
...
be-shivtekha be-veitekha,
u-ve-lekhtekha ba-derekh,
u-ve-shokhbekha u-vqumekha.
U-khtavtam al mezuzot beitekha
u-vish'arekha.

Deut. 11:
... yoreh u-malqosh;
ve-asafta deganekha ve-tiroshekha ve-yitzharekha.
...
be-shivtekha be-veitekha,
u-ve-lekhtekha ba-derekh,
u-ve-shokhbekha u-vqumekha.
U-khtavtam al mezuzot beitekha u-vish'arekha.

Num. 15:
Ve-asu lahem tzitzit ...
Vehaya lakhem le-tzitzit,
u-re'item oto u-zekhartem ...
...
Ani A-donai E-loheikhem ...
Ani A-donai E-loheikhem.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Deut. 6 concentrates singular -kha/-ekha endings and recurring formulas; Deut. 11 embeds the same singular formula in a plural frame and adds malqosh / tirosh-; Num. 15 shifts to tzitzit repetition, shorter sound fragments, and exact divine-name framing.

### 4.2 Genesis 1 - narrative organization across scales

**Status:** CORE
**Reference:** Gen. 1.1-5; Gen. 2.1-3
**Mechanism:** local echo; long-range return; formulaic recurrence; Masoretic phrasing

**Why this example is here.** Genesis 1 tests sound organization inside narrative alongside the chapter's well-known formulaic architecture.

```text
Bereshit bara E-lohim et hashamayim ve'et ha'aretz.
Veha'aretz hayetah tohu vavohu, vechoshekh al penei tehom,
veruach E-lohim merachefet al penei hamayim.

Vayomer E-lohim yehi or, vayehi or.
Vayar E-lohim et ha'or ki tov;
vayavdel E-lohim bein ha'or u-vein hachoshekh.
Vayiqra E-lohim la'or yom, ve-lachoshekh qara laila.
Vayehi erev vayehi voker, yom echad.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Tohu-vohu and yehi or-vayehi or operate locally; tehom-yom supplies a longer-range return; E-lohim formulas and evening-morning closures organize larger spans.

### 4.3 Exodus 34.23-24 - refrain framing and a performance heuristic

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Exod. 34.23-24
**Mechanism:** lexical frame; repeated three-times formula; optional ternary grouping

**Why this example is here.** The repeated shalosh pe'amim ba-shanah frame is textual and immediately visible, making this a useful bridge between repetition and a more tentative performance hypothesis.

```text
Shalosh pe'amim ba-shanah
yera'eh kol-zekhurkha
et-penei ha-Adon A-donai E-lohei Yisrael.
...
ba'alotkha lera'ot
et-penei A-donai E-lohekha
shalosh pe'amim ba-shanah.
```

**What it demonstrates.** The repeated formula creates a cyclical lexical frame; a ternary realization can then be tested independently.

**Interpretive note.** The lexical frame is textual; the ternary realization is a separate performance hypothesis that can be tested experimentally.

### 4.4 The Decalogue - legal authority through anaphora and compact cadence

**Status:** SUPPORTING
**Reference:** Exod. 20.2-14; Deut. 5.6-18
**Mechanism:** Lo-anaphora; short prohibition series; suffixal fields; rhetorical progression

**Why this example is here.** The Decalogue shows how legal force can be carried by repeated negative openings and compressed prohibitions without requiring an extended rhyme scheme.

```text
Anokhi A-donai E-lohekha,
asher hotzeitikha me-eretz Mitzrayim,
mi-beit avadim.

Lo yihyeh lekha E-lohim acheirim al panai.
Lo ta'aseh lekha pesel ...

Lo tirtzach.
Lo tin'af.
Lo tignov.

Deut. 5.18:
Lo tachmod ... ve-lo tit'aveh ...
```

**What it demonstrates.** Lo-anaphora, compact matched prohibitions, and recurring second-person suffixes create formal cohesion.

**Interpretive note.** Tin'af / la-shav belongs to the project's graded f/v correspondence and is not counted as a STRICT identity match.

### 4.5 Leviticus 25.35-38 - economic law as a genre stress test

**Status:** CORE
**Reference:** Lev. 25.35-38
**Mechanism:** recurrent verse closure; syntactic parallelism; singular-to-plural shift

**Why this example is here.** Technical economic law is a strong test of whether formal sound organization extends beyond lyrical or narrative material.

```text
Ve-khi yamukh achikha
u-mata yado immakh,
ve-hechezaqta bo,
ger ve-toshav,
va-chai immakh.

Al tiqach me-ito neshekh ve-tarbit,
ve-yareta me-E-lohekha,
ve-chei achikha immakh.

Et kaspekha
lo titten lo be-neshekh,
u-ve-marbit
lo titten okhlekha.

Ani A-donai E-loheikhem,
asher hotzeiti etkhem me-eretz Mitzrayim,
latet lakhem et eretz Kena'an,
lihyot lakhem le-E-lohim.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Vv. 35 and 36 share the verse-final immakh cadence; v. 37 builds a strict lo titten parallel; v. 38 shifts from singular obligation to a plural covenantal frame.

**Interpretive note.** The broader kh/ch field is a sound field surrounding the stronger verse-closing and syntactic correspondences; it is not treated as one exact rhyme class.

### 4.6 Numbers 18.8-10 - institutional allocation and enumerative sound

**Status:** CORE
**Reference:** Num. 18.8-10
**Mechanism:** pronominal recurrence; enumerative -am sequence; qodesh framing

**Why this example is here.** This legal case tests recurrent sound inside administrative enumeration rather than interpersonal ethics.

```text
Va-yedabber A-donai el Aharon,
va-ani hinne natatti lekha
et mishmeret terumotai,
le-khol qodshei bnei Yisrael,
lekha netattim ...
u-le-vanekha le-choq olam.

Ze yihye lekha mi-qodesh ha-qodashim ...
kol qorbanam,
le-khol minchatam,
u-le-khol chattatam,
u-le-khol ashamam ...
qodesh qodashim lekha hu u-le-vanekha.

Be-qodesh ha-qodashim tokhalenu ...
qodesh yihye lakh.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Lekha marks entitlement points; the -am sequence structures the list; qodesh vocabulary frames the sacred domain.

### 4.7 Deuteronomy 19.15-19 - judicial symmetry without lyric content

**Status:** CORE
**Reference:** Deut. 19.15-19
**Mechanism:** lexical return; numerical progression; reciprocal legal structure; conventional closure

**Why this example is here.** This austere procedural unit tests formal organization where conventional poetic motivation is especially weak.

```text
Lo yaqum ed echad be-ish ...
al pi shnei edim
o al pi shlosha edim
yaqum davar.

Ki yaqum ed chamas be-ish ...
ve-hinne ed sheqer ha-ed,
sheqer ana be-achiv.

Va-asitem lo
ka-asher zamam la-asot le-achiv;
u-viarta ha-ra mi-qirbekha.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Ed changes legal status through the procedure; numerical escalation and reciprocal penalty mirror the legal logic.

## 5. Orientation and Comparative Calibration

These comparative examples serve as auditory calibration. Because their sound organization is especially conspicuous, they make particular mechanisms easy to recognize before subtler Torah instances are considered.

### 5.1 Psalm 40.8-9 - a compact recognized-poetic rhyme network

**Status:** ORIENTATION
**Reference:** Ps. 40.8-9
**Mechanism:** multiple simultaneous end relations in a recognized poetic corpus

**Why this example is here.** A short Psalm unit makes layered rhyme relations easy to hear before similar layering is considered in less obvious contexts.

```text
Az amarti hinne-ba'ti
bimgillat-sefer katuv alai.
La'asot-retzonkha E-lohai chafatzti
ve-toratkha be-tokh me'ai.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Alai / me'ai form a paired ending; ba'ti / chafatzti adds a cross-line relation.

### 5.2 Psalm 104.4-5 - grammatical comparator for marked-form analysis

**Status:** ORIENTATION
**Reference:** Ps. 104.4-5
**Mechanism:** grammatical agreement + graded t/d terminal relation

**Why this example is here.** This comparator belongs conceptually beside the marked Torah forms but remains separated because it is outside the Pentateuch. It shows a third textual level: grammatical agreement rather than qere/ketiv or consonantal spelling.

```text
Ps. 104.4: ... esh lohet |
Ps. 104.5: ... olam va'ed.
```

**What it demonstrates.** The /et/-/ed/ near echo crosses the verse boundary if t/d are treated as a graded consonantal pair.

**Interpretive note.** The relation spans Ps. 104.4-5 and uses the project's graded t/d correspondence.

### 5.3 Ana Be-koach - dense interlaced rhyme as auditory orientation

**Status:** ORIENTATION
**Reference:** Siddur Ashkenaz, Shabbat, Kabbalat Shabbat, Ana Be-koach
**Mechanism:** dense terminal fields; parallel imperatives; percussive cadence

**Why this example is here.** This example is pedagogical rather than evidential for the Torah. Its dense rhyme fields and repeated imperatives make interlaced sound organization unusually easy to hear.

```text
Ana be-koach gedulat yeminkha tatir tzerurah;
Qabel rinat amkha, sagveinu, tahareinu nora;
Na gibor, dorshei yichudkha, kevavat shomrem;
Barkhem, taharem, rachamem, tzidqatkha tamid gamlem;
Chasin qadosh, be-rov tuvkha, nahel adatekha;
Yachid ge'eh, le-amkha p'neh, zokhrei qedushatekha;
Shav'atenu qabel, u-shma tza'aqatenu, yodea ta'alumot;
Barukh shem kevod malkhuto le-olam va'ed.
```

**What it demonstrates.** Several end fields interact with repeated imperatives such as qabel / barkhem / qabel.

**Interpretive note.** The displayed wording follows the Sefaria Siddur Ashkenaz witness used for this catalogue. The value of the example here is its exceptionally dense, easily audible interlacing of rhyme and imperative cadence.
