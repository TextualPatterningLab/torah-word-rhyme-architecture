# Ana BeKoach — Experimental Rhythmic Realization

- **Audio:** `ana_bekoach_experimental.mp3`
- **Text:** *Ana BeKoach*
- **Function in this repository:** comparative rhythmic listening example; not part of the Torah passage-inspection corpus
- **Duration:** approximately 40.3 seconds
- **Format:** MP3, stereo, 44.1 kHz, 128 kbps
- **Cubase project tempo:** 80 BPM
- **Cubase project meter:** 4/4

## Purpose

This recording provides an experimental rhythmic realization of a compact liturgical text with dense recurrent sound patterning and strongly perceptible cadence. It is intended to make recurrent endings, lexical return, parallel imperatives, accentual grouping, and short cadential units easier to hear in continuous performance.

The recording is included as a comparative example only. *Ana BeKoach* is not part of the Torah corpus used by the passage-inspection tool in this repository. The realization is not presented as a reconstruction of an ancient melody, a historically transmitted meter, or independent proof of the proposed Torah analysis. The Cubase tempo and meter describe this particular experimental realization only.

Its value is demonstrative: the compact text makes dense sound recurrence and percussive rhythmic organization especially easy to perceive, providing an auditory point of comparison for the more distributed patterns discussed in the Torah examples.

## Listening transcription

Ana be'koach gedulat yeminekha tatir tzerurah;

Qabel rinat amekha, sagveinu, tahareinu nora;

Na gibbor, dorshei yichudekha, kevavat shomrem;

Barekhem, taharem, rachamei tzidkatekha tamid gamlem;

Chasin qadosh, berov tuv'kha, nahel adatekha;

Yachid ge'eh, le'amkha p'neh, zochrei qedushatekha;

Shav'atenu qabel, ushma' tza'akatenu, yodea ta'alumot;

Baruch shem kevod malkhuto le'olam va'ed.

## Listening points

The recording is intended to make several textually observable relations especially easy to follow:

- the opening **tzerurah / nora** return;
- the dense **-em** field in **shomrem / barekhem / taharem / gamlem**;
- the recurrent second-person **-ekha / -tekha** field across **yeminekha, amekha, yichudekha, tzidkatekha, adatekha, qedushatekha**;
- the lexical return of **qabel** near the beginning and again in the final line of the main acrostic text;
- the repeated imperative syntax and short cadential units that give the passage its strongly percussive flow.

The audio is supplementary and illustrative. The Torah-based claims discussed in the accompanying study do not depend on this particular realization or on *Ana BeKoach* itself.
